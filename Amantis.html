Amantis

From salesperson to campaign designer. Three years inside a brand.

“Behind the counter, I realised the shop’s biggest problem wasn’t going to be solved by selling. It would be solved by designing.”

Type In-store system, two campaigns
Client Amantis
Deliverables Product posters, Vibronove identity (logo, 5-step posters, drop-off bin, landing)
Status In store. 3D display and bin as proposals.
Role Sales, then shop manager. Design and direction of both campaigns.

Beyond the brief. Before the campaign.

Before the campaigns, I started where I was most comfortable: the brand itself. A moving logo and a product box were how I began to understand Amantis as a designer, not from behind the counter.

01

Salesperson first. Designer second.

The problem

I joined as a salesperson. From behind the counter I saw the problem: customers looked at the products without understanding how they worked, and all of that information lived in us. Every answer depended on someone being there to give it, and in this category, asking out loud is already a friction.

The system is a template. Any product slots in: swap the specs and the callouts, keep the rest.

Tools : After Effects · Photoshop · Figma · Blender · Illustrator ·
02

A good idea, abandoned in a corner of the site.

Vibronove

Vibronove already existed: a sex-toy recycling campaign with a real idea under it, but no system, no image and no one to switch it on. Recycling here has two limits, the product is intimate and the process can’t promise everything, so I moved the message to what the customer actually controls.

03

The system worked. People had to switch it on.

A system like this depends on someone bringing it into the conversation. As shop manager, I trained the sales team to mention Vibronove at the right moment. That’s when the loop appeared: the customer recycled, came back with the discount, and often ended up becoming a member.
